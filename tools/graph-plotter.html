Practical graph plotter

Type in your results, or pick an example. The independent variable (the one you change) goes along the bottom, and the dependent variable (the one you measure) goes up the side. Look at the pattern, then choose a line of best fit. Tap a point to leave it out.

Why?

Axes. Label each axis with the quantity and its unit, for example “Force / N” or “Force in N”. The independent variable goes along the bottom.

Scales. Go up in steps of 1, 2 or 5 (or 10, 20, 50…), never 3s, and make the points cover at least half the grid each way. The axes don’t have to start at zero: this one starts at zero only when the points still fill at least half of it.

Plotting. Plot each point as a small, neat cross, to within half a small square. Use a sharp pencil.

A line of best fit is one thin straight line or smooth curve through the middle of the points, with about as many above it as below. Never join the dots.

Anomalous results are a long way from the line. Ignore them when you draw it, and repeat them if you can.

The gradient of a straight line = change in y ÷ change in x. Draw a big triangle, using at least half of the line, so your readings are more accurate.

Directly proportional means a straight line that goes through the origin: double one, and the other doubles too.
