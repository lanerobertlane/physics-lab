Contamination, irradiation & background

We are exposed to a little radiation all the time: this is background radiation. Being near a radioactive source irradiates you, but you don’t become radioactive. If radioactive atoms get on or in you, you are contaminated, and they keep irradiating you after you walk away.

Correcting for background

A Geiger–Müller tube always picks up some background radiation. To find the count rate from a source alone, measure the background first and take it away.

Contamination or irradiation?

Your yearly dose

Radiation dose is measured in sieverts (Sv). 1 millisievert (mSv) = 0.001 Sv. It measures the risk of harm to the body.

Background radiation comes from natural sources (rocks, radon gas, cosmic rays, food) and man-made sources (medical scans, fallout from nuclear weapons testing and nuclear accidents).

Which is most dangerous?

To stay safe: keep sources in lead-lined boxes, handle them with tongs, keep your distance and keep the time short. People who work with radiation wear a dosimeter badge.

Remember

Irradiation: being exposed to radiation from a source outside you. It stops when you move away, and you don’t become radioactive.

Contamination: radioactive atoms get on or into something. They keep decaying and irradiating it until they are removed or decay away.
