Nuclear fission & fusion

Fission: a large, unstable nucleus such as uranium-235 absorbs a neutron and splits into two smaller nuclei, giving out 2 or 3 neutrons, gamma rays and a lot of energy. Fusion: two light nuclei join to make a heavier one, releasing energy. This is what powers the Sun.

Reaction

Chain reaction: each fission gives out 2 or 3 neutrons, which can split more nuclei. In a nuclear reactor, control rods absorb some of the neutrons so that, on average, only one goes on to cause another fission: the reaction is controlled. In a nuclear weapon the chain reaction is uncontrolled and causes an explosion.

All the fission products move off fast: they have kinetic energy, which heats the reactor.

Fusion

Nuclei are positive, so they repel each other. They only get close enough to join if they are moving very fast, which needs a very high temperature (and, in the Sun, a very high pressure too).

The helium nucleus and neutron have slightly less mass than the two hydrogen nuclei. The missing mass has been converted into energy.
