Earth, Sun & Moon

The Earth spins on its axis once a day, giving day and night. The axis is tilted by 23.4°, and this tilt, together with the Earth’s orbit round the Sun, gives the seasons: the Sun climbs higher in summer, so its light is more concentrated and the days are longer. The Moon’s phases come from how much of its sunlit half we can see.

Time and date

The Sun’s light is strongest where it is overhead. Near the poles the same light hits the ground at a low angle, so it is spread out and heats the ground less.

Summer is warmer for two reasons: the Sun is higher, so each m² of ground gets more energy, and the days are longer. It is not because the Earth is closer to the Sun (it is actually closest in January).

Moon

The Moon doesn’t make its own light: half of it is always lit by the Sun. As it orbits the Earth (about once every 29½ days), we see different amounts of that lit half.
