Static electricity & electric fields

Rubbing two insulators together moves electrons from one to the other. The one that gains electrons becomes negatively charged; the one that loses them is left positively charged. Like charges repel, unlike charges attract.

Only electrons move, never positive charges. Polythene pulls electrons off a cloth; acetate loses electrons to the cloth.

Balloon

Drag the balloon back and forth over the jumper to rub it. Then drag it to the wall and let go.

The wall isn’t charged, but the balloon’s charge pushes the electrons in the wall’s surface slightly away. The surface nearest the balloon becomes slightly positive, and attracts it.

Electric fields

A charged object has an electric field around it. Another charge placed in the field feels a force. The lines show the direction of the force on a small positive charge: away from +, towards −.

The field is strongest close to the charge, where the lines are closest together. Drag the small test charge to see the force on it.

A strong enough field can pull electrons off air particles, making a spark.
