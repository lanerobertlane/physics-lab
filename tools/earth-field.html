Earth’s magnetic field

A magnetic compass contains a small bar magnet. Find out which way it points, what can change that, and why it tells us that the core of the Earth must be magnetic.

Why the core must be magnetic

Which pole is at the Arctic? The north pole of a compass is attracted to a south pole. So the magnetic pole near the North Pole is really a magnetic south pole.

The core isn’t really a bar magnet, because it’s far too hot to stay magnetised. The field comes from molten iron moving around in the outer core. The bar magnet is a model that shows the shape of the field. In the UK the real field also tilts steeply down into the ground, but a compass held flat can only show the direction along the ground.

How a compass works

The needle is a small bar magnet on a pivot, so it’s free to turn. The red end is its north pole. It lines up with the magnetic field where it is, so it points in the direction of the field.
