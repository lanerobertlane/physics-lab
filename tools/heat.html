Conduction, convection & radiation

Energy is transferred by heating in three ways. Conduction passes it through solids, particle to particle. Convection carries it round in liquids and gases. Radiation sends it as infrared waves, which can even cross empty space.

Particles at the hot end vibrate more. They bump into their neighbours and pass the energy along. Metals are the best conductors because they also have free electrons that carry energy quickly through the metal.

How convection works

  1. The water near the heater gets warmer.
  2. Its particles move faster and spread out, so the warm water becomes less dense.
  3. The less dense warm water rises. Cooler, denser water sinks to take its place.
  4. This flow is a convection current. It carries energy all round the water.

Convection only happens in fluids (liquids and gases), because their particles can move from place to place. It's how a radiator heats a whole room.

Cans

Dark, matt surfaces are the best absorbers (and emitters) of infrared. Light, shiny surfaces reflect most of it. Radiation doesn't need particles, so it works through a vacuum: that's how energy from the Sun reaches the Earth.
