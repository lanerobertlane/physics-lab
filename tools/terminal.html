Terminal velocity

A skydiver jumps from 3000 m. Watch the weight and air resistance arrows as they fall. When the two forces are equal, the skydiver falls at a steady speed: their terminal velocity. Open the parachute to slow down to a safe landing speed.

Velocity–time graph

The gradient of the graph is the acceleration. It starts at 9.8 m/s² and falls to zero as the air resistance grows. Where the line is flat, the skydiver is at terminal velocity.

Forces

Air resistance gets bigger as the speed goes up. The resultant force is the weight minus the air resistance.

The skydiver

Body position

A heavier skydiver needs more air resistance to balance their weight, so their terminal velocity is higher. A streamlined dive has less air resistance at the same speed.
