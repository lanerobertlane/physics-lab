Mains electricity

Mains electricity in the UK is an alternating supply at 230 V and 50 Hz. See a.c. and d.c. on an oscilloscope, wire a three-pin plug, and find out how the earth wire and the fuse keep you safe.

What happens

Oscilloscope

Why does the trace peak at 325 V, not 230 V? The mains p.d. is always changing, and it spends most of each cycle below its peak. 230 V is a kind of average: it heats things as much as a steady 230 V d.c. supply would. To manage that, the a.c. has to swing higher, up to about 325 V (230 × √2).

The three wires

  • Live (brown): carries the alternating p.d. from the supply, about 230 V. It goes through the fuse.
  • Neutral (blue): completes the circuit, at or close to 0 V.
  • Earth (green and yellow): a safety wire at 0 V. It only carries current if there is a fault.

Set-up

Fuse

The live wire is dangerous even when a switch is off, because it is still connected to the supply. A p.d. of 230 V between your body (at 0 V, the earth) and the live wire makes a current flow through you.
